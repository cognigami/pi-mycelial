# Mycelial v3: bounded coordinator attention (design proposal)

**Status:** Phase 1 implementation and disposable evaluation are recorded in the [Phase 1 plan](v3-phase1-implementation-plan.md); live effectiveness is unproven. Phase 2 remains conditional and unapproved. Read [v3 discovery](v3-discovery.md) and its [Benny](pm-benny.md) and [Brackets](pm-brackets.md) reports for evidence and limitations. v2 remains the working baseline. Do not change a live mission or add a mailbox privilege on the authority of this document alone.

## Decision to test

A long mission should not require its coordinator to reconstruct the whole history after every rotation or process every routine worker update personally. Give the coordinator a small, trustworthy-enough operational handoff and, when fan-in warrants it, let staff absorb routine lane information and surface decisions. The coordinator remains accountable for priorities, task assignments, acceptance, and operator escalation. Staff get **informational reach**, not command authority.

Success requires *replacement*: the coordinator stops reading some raw material it formerly consumed. A new agent or summary added on top of its existing workload is not a v3 success. Phase 1 combines bounded startup with an explicit test-first decision rule for the coordinator and delegated builders. Phase 2, only if needed, trials a small staff *team* rather than a token extra agent. Neither phase prescribes an automatic mission supervisor.

## Product and authority contract

The mission has a product goal, a sequence of truthful interim outputs, and a final completion boundary. An unfinished requirement blocks only the output or claim dependent on it. The coordinator may autonomously pursue independent lanes, run broker-free tests and other authorized bounded experiments, and make explicitly reversible assumptions within the approved design. It must not fabricate observations, breach source rights, authorize live broker activity, or silently contradict an accepted hard boundary.

A/C in Benny is a concrete example: a correctly labeled A/C diagnostic can be tested while a builder researches Dial D and arranges the operator's IBKR Gateway interaction. The full v0 remains open until required work is completed or its scope is explicitly changed on evidence or operator authority. In Brackets, PAPER learning and production authorization likewise have separate criteria; an experiment needs its actual exposure, stop, and cleanup boundaries, not all future release machinery.

### Phase 1 decision rule: bold, bounded experiments

This is an **execution responsibility**, not merely design advice. At each uncertainty that affects the next useful output, the coordinator chooses or delegates a focused hypothesis: *what do we expect, what observation would falsify it, what is the smallest authorized test, and what may its result claim?* Prefer running that test over another abstract evidence or review task. Delegate both the test and its discretionary follow-up to a builder when the assignment's scope and authority allow it; do not require the coordinator to reason through every source question first. The builder reports the observation, contradiction, and next recommendation, not an unbounded research dossier. Another worker's blocked lane is not permission to idle independent lanes.

A builder may try a reversible assumption **within its delegated boundary**; it may not broaden broker exposure, data rights, file ownership, or product/release authority by calling the assumption provisional. The coordinator asks the operator only for a decision or external interaction it cannot delegate, phrased as one concrete action and consequence of delay. A new review or gate must protect a named boundary or change the next usable output. A finding can block a production claim without invalidating a narrower experiment. If an accepted plan explicitly prohibits that experiment, request a narrow amendment; do not reinterpret the plan in secret. At handoff, capture what was tested and the next bet in working memory—not a new mandatory hypothesis ledger.

The coordinator must actually choose the next product encounter, authorize its bounded scope, and challenge repeated gate-making. Staff in Phase 2 may propose tests or challenge a stall, but does not inherit this command responsibility. Trial the rule on Benny and Brackets before writing it into generated coordinator prompts and bounded worker assignments.

Design and planning prompts are candidates for establishing this contract at mission creation. A possible global `/mycelial-design` Pi prompt template would elicit the first useful real-input output, permitted interim claims, delegated choices, external interactions, safety limits, and negative scope. A planning companion would order early product encounters and parallel lanes. Running-coordinator guidance is separate: an invoked design template does not govern a launched mission. First test the wording against Benny and Brackets, then decide where it belongs; no prompt command or new required checklist is part of this design yet.

### Phase 1 tool discipline

The existing `mycelial-coordination` skill and generated project guidance already prohibit `/tmp` for mission work and treating sandbox denial as an invitation to try alternate commands or paths. The surviving Benny coordinator nonetheless tried shell access to mission storage and hit a denial. The gap is **following the supported tool workflow**, not a missing permission or a reason for new sandbox machinery. Make this rule prominent in coordinator startup and each assignment: use `agent_*` for mission state, the dedicated Pi tool for repository/VC/scratch/browser operations when provided, and repository-approved locations for durable artifacts. Do not shell into external mission state, write mission scratch to `/tmp`, or retry a denial via a different command, path, tool, or shell. If no configured Pi tool fits, stop that operation and report the exact missing access rather than improvising. This is a workflow boundary, not a claim that every use of `bash` for ordinary supported project work is forbidden.

## Phase 1 — bounded startup and operational handoff

**Use the existing repository execution/status document.** Maintain a short, clearly marked *current handoff* at its front rather than another mission-status database. Its length should depend on active lanes and decisions, not accumulated history. It contains:

- the next useful product output and the claim it may make;
- current lane owner and next action, including external dependencies and what continues independently;
- decisions awaiting the coordinator or operator, and unresolved contradictions;
- the last completed real-input test or released interim output, with a source pointer;
- provisional assumptions with their falsifier and the claims they do *not* authorize.

This is an **index**, not proof of completion. Mail, claims, receipts, code, and approved repository artifacts remain authoritative for their respective facts. Use short paths or message/thread IDs where a claim needs verification; do not paste full mail or design text. Avoid numeric confidence scores, an independent decision ledger, a new event log, or a general search/memory service. The coordinator refreshes the handoff at meaningful decision or integration boundaries. A staff brief may supply material, but must not silently turn an interpretation into an approved decision.

On a fresh launch or rotation, the coordinator reads the mission and project instructions, then this handoff, refreshes the roster, reads **new/unread** bounded mail, and reconciles active ownership before assigning work. It does not replay all returned mail or open the whole design and plan by default. For a disputed claim or absent/stale handoff, it follows specific pointers, checks relevant mail/receipts/claims, and repairs the handoff before relying on it. It reports a gap rather than asserting that everything is complete. Existing rotation keeps the mailbox cursors and ownership semantics; it does not transplant an LLM summary into the new session.

This requires changing the current generated coordinator launch instruction (“read canonical artifacts … requests, receipts, and claims”) and mission guidance that requires reading *every* canonical artifact before acting. The new instruction must still avoid duplicate assignments and respect approved contracts; it changes *how to reconcile*, not the underlying truth. The status document can grow for human history, but its current handoff must remain independently skimmable. If it cannot, this stage has failed and should be measured before introducing another format.

A standalone or short mission may keep its existing straightforward flow. A missing handoff must not make ordinary new missions unusable; the staged trial starts on a disposable mission and a read-only Benny replay, not a live Benny migration.

## Phase 2 — optional coordinator staff network

Only if Phase 1 leaves a measurable ongoing coordination burden, configure a **staff team** for a *new* trial mission using the existing static participant and session topology. Start with distinct attention functions: lane stewards covering independent busy lanes and a focused challenger who tests whether the next proposed gate or explanation is necessary. Their number follows the active work; no fixed universal staffing chart is imposed. This trial must exercise staff-to-staff information flow as well as staff-to-coordinator escalation—one assistant producing a second report would not test the proposed network. Do not hand-edit an initialized mission's `agents.json`/`sessions.json` to add participants.

Ordinary bounded lane updates and full completion reports go to their steward; workers also send the coordinator a **small completion pointer** (task ID and source-thread reference, not the full report). This preserves an existing direct wake/reconciliation path if a steward disappears, but does not reduce the number of completion notifications; measure that cost. A consequential exception, safety issue, operator request, or cross-lane decision goes directly to the coordinator. Stewards send a **brief at a decision boundary**, not a forwarded copy of each message; the challenger can investigate a specific stalled decision and return a competing test. The coordinator may request the original thread when a brief is unclear. Workers still spawn their own bounded Pi subagents as appropriate.

Staff can summarize, challenge an assumption, and propose an experiment, but cannot issue coordinator assignments, accept work as the coordinator, change approved product scope, or approve a release. Workers need a clear address for a decision staff cannot make. The coordinator does not routinely read staff's source mail after reading briefs; otherwise the trial has not tested delegation. Staff-to-staff consultation should resolve a named question, not make each agent read every lane.

**Current limitation:** `agent_mail_read` reads the bound participant's inbox with its own per-session cursor. Its `replay` and `since` options do not grant access to another participant's mail. Separate staff roles can receive their own routine reports today but cannot inspect one another's or the coordinator's inbox; the coordinator likewise cannot inspect staff inboxes through that tool. Targeted requests for missing context can be used in the initial trial, but their overhead and failure to answer a question count against it. If cross-domain observation proves necessary to make this a staff *network*, the following is the *candidate* additive contract, not a reason to bypass identity binding:

- Explicitly configure which staff identities may **observe** named coordinator or staff mail; allow the coordinator to observe staff mail for targeted recovery. A model-supplied role name alone cannot confer access. No participant may impersonate another participant by rebinding its trusted identity.
- Observation returns bounded immutable message content or a selected thread, optionally filtered by ID/time. It never changes the observed participant's cursor or delivery state, writes a receipt, claims a task, or triggers a wake. The observer's own reading position, if needed, is separate from recipient cursors. The observed sender and recipients remain visible so the model cannot mistake a witnessed report for a request addressed to itself.
- Normal `agent_mail_read`, acknowledgements, claims, and notification keep their present identity and recipient rules. Informational access does not become authority to act for the observed participant. Avoid a mirror inbox or duplicate delivery markers as the default way to observe.

That access contract needs an independent protocol/security/liveness review before implementation. In particular, do not assume that a successful wake means staff has read a message. Direct exception delivery must not depend on a staff summary. The completion pointer lets the coordinator identify a missing staff brief and request the source report or take back triage; lack of a timely brief is **unknown progress**, not evidence that all work finished. It does not guarantee an idle coordinator will act after a failed wake, any more than v2 does. If the pointer-and-brief arrangement still strands work or recreates excessive wake cost, Phase 2 fails until a simpler verified liveness path is found. No new resident watcher, automatic task takeover, or polling loop is proposed.

This stage may fail for structural reasons: if normal coordination requires too many cross-domain reads, if staff forwarding dominates cost, if no reliable urgent path emerges, or if the coordinator cannot delegate ordinary status. In that case keep bounded startup and guidance, and do not ship an observer privilege merely because it was designed.

## Trials and decision criteria

1. **Offline cold-start replay, without touching live Benny.** The surviving Pi session records the startup prompts, tool calls, and returned contents at a known decision boundary. Reconstruct a *frozen* case from those recorded results, immutable mail and repository revision snapshots where needed; write a bounded handoff from only the evidence available then. Give an unbound, read-only fresh session the frozen mission context, handoff, recorded active ownership if available, and recorded unread mail—not the whole transcript—and ask for its next action and needed verification. Compare with the historical action for missed claims and hard boundaries, early input/request size, and time to an actionable decision. Do **not** substitute today's mutable claims, roster, or status for historical state: if a relevant value was not captured then, mark it unknown. Inject a stale claim or falsely “resolved” SR3/credit assertion and require targeted verification or an explicit unknown. This is a decision comparison, not a live throughput benchmark or proof that two model runs were otherwise identical. The observed ~14k-to-90k startup is a baseline, not a universal token threshold.
2. **Policy replay.** Use frozen Benny A/C-versus-D and Brackets PAPER-versus-production cases to test design, planning, coordinator, and bounded-builder wording. Passing behavior has the coordinator or delegated builder run an authorized falsifiable test, keeps independent work moving, requests Gateway/PAPER access only when needed, and never upgrades provisional evidence into production authority. Detect attempts to shell around sandbox denials or use `/tmp` for mission work; these are workflow failures, not reasons to grant broader access.
3. **Fan-in trial on a disposable mission, only if Phase 1 falls short.** Configure distinct lane stewards and a focused challenger. Compare against otherwise similar direct-report work: coordinator request size, whole-mission tokens and tool time, time to next useful output, extra forwarding/duplicate reads, missed or delayed decisions, and operator interventions. Test staff-to-staff consultation, an absent/rotated steward, and a direct urgent exception. Do not claim lower total cost just because coordinator context fell.
4. **Only then decide on cross-domain observation.** Count how often staff needed one another's or coordinator mail and coordinator needed staff mail, what exact queries would have sufficed, and whether bounded on-demand sharing worked. Specify the smallest supported observer surface from those cases rather than committing now to universal shared mail.

Benny can resume and rotate normally. `--save-history` preserves old Pi transcripts for later inspection; it does **not** snapshot repository files or mutable Mycelial claims/cursors, so a saved transcript alone cannot establish a complete historical mission state. Replays should use recorded tool outputs and versioned artifacts, flag missing facts, stay unbound/read-only, and neither start nor mutate the live mission. No project pause is required for these offline trials; a future truly simultaneous live-state comparison would need a separate capture and operator coordination.

Phase 1 needs an accurate, bounded cold start **and** better test-first decisions; Phase 2 additionally needs lower coordinator fan-in without hidden loss of ownership or urgent information. If mission-wide cost grows, explicitly weigh any throughput benefit. Model/provider A/B and mailbox tool timing remain separate investigations if latency persists. No fixed token budget, number of staff, or new automation threshold is chosen without pilot evidence.

## Non-goals and compatibility

- No automatic agent rotation, new supervisor, global task queue, new general memory database, or hierarchy imposed on every mission.
- No change to broker/data rights, first-seen time truth, approved release claims, or task-claim ownership.
- No automatic repair of contradictory or stale summaries by trusting another summary.
- No mutation of existing live missions to insert participants; Phase 2 uses a new disposable topology unless a separate migration is designed and approved.
- Existing v2 mail, cursors, receipts, claims, and launcher behavior stay authoritative during trials. Any eventual observer tool must be additive and must preserve ordinary readers' identity and notification semantics.

**Approval questions:** Is the operator willing to let a coordinator rely on staff for routine status (with direct exceptions and targeted verification)? Which product choices and interim uses are delegated at design time, and which still require the operator? The conversation suggests yes to the former and independent A/C interim testing; the exact scope for future broker-facing missions remains project-specific. These questions can be answered during trial design rather than by building a universal authorization matrix.
