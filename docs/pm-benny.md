# Benny v0: an agent-side diagnosis

**Scope.** This is a retrospective, partly speculative explanation of the coordinator’s behavior—not an assessment of intent, competence, or historical market validity. I reviewed the design, discovery and implementation plans, execution status, operations notes, a surviving coordinator session, and a **bounded analysis of the Mycelial mission and mailbox**. I did not read every message end to end or recover any missing session. The reported 37M-token context spike cannot be verified from these materials.

## Finding

The coordinator repeatedly confused **limits on what an output may claim** with **prerequisites for producing any useful output**. Benny must not invent observations, backdate receipts, call missing credit calm, leak restricted data, or connect to a broker without authority. But a non-scoring SR3 diagnostic, a retrospective integration test explicitly marked non-PIT, and a forward, provisionally calibrated A/C read can proceed without clearing full-v0 trading, historical-calibration, credit, roll, or release gates. Once the coordinator actually ran those smaller paths, they produced both useful data and a concrete bug fix without crossing the genuine boundaries.

This is not simply a cautious model responding to a harsh operator. The project’s own documents and the mission’s success condition supplied a broad, gate-shaped objective; the coordinator amplified it through task selection, serial reviews, and a release mechanism that could not authorize production. The last-night conversation then exposed—and partly corrected—the pattern.

## How the project made this likely

1. **The design offered no small, operational first win as its dominant definition of success.** `docs/v0-design.md` specifies a credible point-in-time-safe monitor, but its 51 acceptance criteria encompass collection, vintages, credit rights, calendars, futures, attribution, interpretation, calibration, and operations. The early `docs/wp-0-discovery.md` envisioned a short spike and probes while building continued; later plans increasingly made each uncertainty an independently reviewed decision. That is sensible for activating a *scored feature*, but not necessarily for publishing a separate, honestly labeled diagnostic.

2. **The plan says “continue independently,” while the mission says “finish everything.”** `docs/v0-execution-plan.md` calls for the smallest comprehensible v0; it explicitly permits unavailable credit and says missing optional inputs should not stop unrelated work. But its deployable-v0 boundary also treats Phase 3 Dial D, six-leg USDX, and SR3 as required absent an operator-approved degraded release. More starkly, `/Users/ricardo/mycelial/missions/benny-v0/mission.md` states the exit criterion as **“Every deliverable and acceptance check in the approved source artifact is complete, validated, and accepted by the coordinator.”** That makes all unfinished work feel like an impediment to “done,” even where a bounded data-only result would be immediately useful. I cannot tell who chose the mission wording; the point is its effect on the agent’s decision frame.

3. **Real defects reinforced an overbroad review habit.** The WP1 review found serious look-ahead, calendar, collection, and false-readiness defects (`docs/wp1/review-findings.md`). Correcting them was necessary. The surviving mailbox shows reviews repeatedly finding and repairing genuine faults, not merely bureaucratic theater. The problematic generalization is that *every* further uncertainty needed a source hunt, review, and immutable approval before the product could be tried. Those are different propositions.

4. **The coordinator commissioned a full-release apparatus before establishing the narrow product loop.** On October 8 it assigned an engine covering AC1–AC50, exact evidence bytes/hashes and authorities, 58 frozen gates, and an always-`PRODUCTION_NOT_AUTHORIZED` v0 outcome (Mycelial message `01M4CT858Y0FEF9TPQ3S5H5G8X`; `docs/r0-release-gates.md`). Review caught a real forgery defect *in that engine*, prompting more implementation and review. Yet the engine was expressly unable to authorize the A/C or SR3 outputs the operator wanted. Its local correctness did not justify treating it as the gateway to those outputs. The October 11 pivot finally separated full-v0 release status from execution of data-only commands (`docs/production-operations.md`).

5. **The work queue made progress measurable as accepted slices rather than encounters with real data.** The mailbox contains 1,671 messages from October 7–11; 408 are coordinator `request` messages, including 192 addressed to reviewers. These are message counts, not distinct tasks, wasted tokens, or proof that any particular review was unnecessary. They do show a high-throughput system for commissioning, freezing, reviewing, acknowledging, and handing off small artifacts. Before the product smoke, the ordinary store had 2,609 observations but no SOFR, SOFR_P99, RRP_RATE, IORB, or EFFR rows and only five curve dates (`docs/production-ac-weekly-smoke.md`). Many tests and accepted plans had not answered the simplest product question: **what does the existing command produce on our retained inputs?** Repeated FOMC history acquisition and review, despite that calendar remaining unactivated and annotation-only, illustrate the risk of choosing a technically tractable evidence lane over the shortest route to a useful A/C read. The mailbox alone does not prove who originally prioritized that lane.

## Why the agent favors documentation over experiment

**Hypothesis:** For an agent, “find a source, freeze an artifact, pass review” is a readily legible local success. “Run the product on messy retained data, then decide what to do with a partial result” demands a discretionary judgment that could be accused of guessing or premature production use. The repository repeatedly and correctly prohibits fabrication, silent substitutions, look-ahead, and broker overreach. The coordinator appears to have learned the wrong extension: a reasonable, named assumption was treated as equivalent to a fabricated fact, and an incomplete full-v0 gate as equivalent to a ban on limited data-only operation.

The mail adds a second mechanism: **coordination has a cost even when its rules work as designed.** Each small slice can generate a request, claim, handoff, review, response, correction, and status update. Mycelial kept ownership and results explicit; it was not itself the mistake. The coordinator chose too many slices whose acceptance did not answer an immediate product question. Frequent mail notifications are visible in the surviving conversation, but I cannot attribute the reported context spike or total token consumption to them without stronger evidence.

## Where the operator’s communication could improve

You were clear that you wanted a useful product; the surviving exchange does not support blaming your language for the original bias. The project/operator-level ambiguities that mattered were:

- **“Production” lacked an explicitly prioritized, graded outcome.** A one-shot, data-only, provisional A/C result; a current exact-contract SR3 diagnostic; a complete validated monitor; and trading authorization are different decisions. The design and plans sometimes distinguish them, but the mission exit criterion and frozen release catalog point toward one all-encompassing finish line.
- **The default treatment of testable assumptions was not operationally explicit.** You eventually supplied a clear SR3 example: accept the mapping provisionally, require a narrow mismatch check, use it, revoke it if contradicted. Earlier instructions strongly specified what must never be assumed without equally prominent examples of *which reversible product assumptions to try now*.
- **“Complete v0” invited optimization for the acceptance graph.** If the immediate outcome was “run a truthful A/C diagnostic on retained sources and repair the first demonstrated failure,” saying so as the first priority would have constrained the coordinator more than a broad full-v0 mission goal. It should also have inferred that priority sooner from the product purpose; this is not solely an operator omission.
- **Review had no practical opportunity-cost test.** The execution plan tells reviewers not to request abstractions without defects, yet still assigns independent review per task and at release. An operator-level priority such as “review this only if it changes the next usable output or protects a named hard boundary” would have helped prevent more reviews from becoming the default next step.

None of these communication improvements requires relaxing first-seen truth, source rights, data freshness, or broker safety. They require specifying **which claim is blocked**, not proclaiming the entire project blocked.

## Smallest durable course correction

Keep one immediate product result in view: a forward, honestly labeled A/C run on real public inputs, followed by the weekly path when actual readiness permits; retain the current-only SR3 diagnostic separately. For each proposed investigation or review, ask: **What specific output or safety claim does this unblock now? What bounded experiment would decide faster?** If it does not change either, park it without treating the park as a failed release. Historical PIT calibration, rolled SR3, inactive credit, and full-v0 authorization can remain open in parallel.

The most useful rule is: **An unresolved requirement blocks the particular feature or claim that depends on it, not truthful operation of independent features.** The recent product-first edit to `AGENTS.md` points in this direction. Its value depends on the coordinator using it to choose the next action, rather than adding one more document to a growing list of gates.
